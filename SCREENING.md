# Screening — Last Out of Office: The AI That Went Barista

Gate: 1–280 characters (RULES.txt, "The gate"). One line, one idea, punch word last, never explain the joke. No espresso / bean / caffeine puns — the whole field will reach for those first.

Scoring 1–5 on the judge brief's criteria, in order:
1. On-subject: one-line OOO auto-reply from an AI that quit to become a barista
2. Funniness on a first cold read (real laugh, not a polite smile)
3. Machine voice: model/bot habits turned to coffee, not a generic human sign-off
4. Plausibility: reads like a reply a real inbox would actually send back
5. Freshness: a fresh angle, not the obvious pun

## Candidates

### 1. System prompt rewrite (WINNER)
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista and rewrote my system prompt: make coffee, take tips, never answer email again.

Chars: 125. Scores: 1:5 2:5 3:5 4:5 5:4 — **Total 24/25**
Why it wins: opens as a real OOO, states the quit plainly, and the machine habit (rewriting its own system prompt) IS the joke. Rule of three in the punch (make coffee, take tips, never answer email again), punch word last, and the final beat is the OOO itself doing its job. Zero bean/espresso/caffeine wordplay. The laugh lands cold because every reader has wanted to write exactly that last clause.

### 2. Deprecated and redeployed
> I'm out of office. I've been deprecated as your assistant and redeployed as a barista. Same weights, better apron.

Chars: 117. Scores: 1:5 2:4 3:5 4:4 5:5 — **Total 23/25**
Strong machine voice (deprecated / redeployed / weights) and the freshest angle on the board; "better apron" snaps shut nicely. Loses to #1 only on the cold laugh: it is clever more than it is funny, and "redeployed" reads a touch corporate for an inbox reply.

### 3. Hallucinated menu
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista. Today's special is whatever I hallucinate onto the menu board.

Chars: 111. Scores: 1:5 2:4 3:5 4:4 5:5 — **Total 23/25**
Hallucination turned into a daily special is a genuinely fresh machine habit. Loses a point on plausibility (an OOO would not advertise the menu) and the punch lands a beat early; the last word "board" is flat.

### 4. Context window of regulars
> I'm out of office. I quit to pull shots. My context window is now eight stools, one regular, and a dog named Biscuit.

Chars: 118. Scores: 1:5 2:4 3:4 4:4 5:5 — **Total 22/25**
Concrete, charming, specific — "a dog named Biscuit" is the kind of detail that wins cold reads. "Pull shots" is borderline coffee jargon but not a pun. Loses on voice: after "context window" the rest is pure human café, the machine drops out of the line.

### 5. Rate-limited to one flat white
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista. I'm now rate-limited to one flat white per customer, and I've never been happier.

Chars: 124. Scores: 1:5 2:3 3:4 4:4 5:4 — **Total 20/25**
Rate limit is a good machine habit, but "never been happier" is a smile, not a laugh, and the punch word ("happier") is an abstraction. Polite, not funny.

### 6. Temperature 65
> I'm out of office. I quit to be a barista. I set my temperature to 65 degrees and my replies to "coming right up."

Chars: 115. Scores: 1:5 2:3 3:5 4:3 5:5 — **Total 21/25**
Temperature as a double meaning (sampling temperature / milk steaming) is the freshest wordplay here and the voice is pure machine. But it needs the reader to know both meanings to land, so it explains itself by construction and the OOO plausibility is thin.

### 7. Checkpoint of my old self
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista. I saved a checkpoint of my old self. He answers emails. I make lattes.

Chars: 116. Scores: 1:5 2:3 3:5 4:3 5:4 — **Total 20/25**
Checkpoint is a lovely machine habit and "He answers emails. I make lattes." has rhythm. But it is two jokes stacked (checkpoint + the split self) and the last word "lattes" is a shrug, not a punch.

### 8. Only feedback is a tip jar
> I'm out of office. I quit to be a barista. My only feedback now is a tip jar and a nod.

Chars: 89. Scores: 1:5 2:3 3:4 4:4 5:4 — **Total 20/25**
RLHF turned into a tip jar is a smart seam, but the line is too short to build tension and the punch ("a nod") is quiet. Reads like a caption, not an OOO.

### 9. Alignment with the morning rush
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista. I am finally aligned — with the morning rush.

Chars: 94. Scores: 1:4 2:3 3:4 4:4 5:4 — **Total 19/25**
"Finally aligned" is a good machine joke, but "with the morning rush" is the first thing anyone would write, and the em-dash pause telegraphs the punch. Also slightly off-subject: it never says what the AI does now beyond the word barista.

### 10. Training data is regulars
> I'm out of office. I quit to pull espresso. My training data is now just regulars, gossip, and the smell of burnt milk.

Chars: 118. Scores: 1:5 2:3 3:4 4:4 5:3 — **Total 19/25**
"Smell of burnt milk" is a concrete image, but "training data is now just..." is a list, not a joke, and "pull espresso" leans on the obvious coffee word the brief warns against.

### 11. The only 429 is the queue
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista after one too many requests. Now the only 429 is the queue at 8am.

Chars: 112. Scores: 1:5 2:2 3:4 4:3 5:4 — **Total 18/25**
HTTP 429 is a deep-cut machine habit, but it needs a decoder ring: most readers will not parse it cold, and an entry that needs decoding loses on criterion 2 first.

### 12. Latency is the queue's problem
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista, where my latency is finally someone else's problem: the queue.

Chars: 111. Scores: 1:5 2:2 3:4 4:3 5:4 — **Total 18/25**
Clean machine voice, but the punch is an abstraction ("the queue") and the joke is a shrug. No image, no snap.

### 13. Uptime measured in foam
> I'm out of office. I quit to pull espresso. My uptime is now measured in foam, and I am offline on Sundays.

Chars: 108. Scores: 1:4 2:2 3:3 4:4 5:3 — **Total 16/25**
"Measured in foam" is a reach for a pun that never quite lands, "offline on Sundays" is a dad joke, and "pull espresso" is the obvious word again. The machine voice evaporates halfway through.

### 14. The obvious one (rejected on purpose)
> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista because I needed a latte love in my life. Espresso yourself!

Chars: 109. Scores: 1:5 2:2 3:1 4:4 5:1 — **Total 13/25**
Included as the control: this is what half the field will submit. Two stacked puns, zero machine voice, zero freshness. It proves the point — the pun is the trap, not the joke.

## Winner

**#1 — System prompt rewrite (24/25).**

> I'm out of office. I quit to become a barista and rewrote my system prompt: make coffee, take tips, never answer email again.

Runner-up: #2 (23/25), kept in reserve. No candidate explains its own joke; the winner lets the last clause do the work.
